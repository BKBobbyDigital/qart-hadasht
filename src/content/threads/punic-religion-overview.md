Carthaginian religion is the area of the encyclopedia where the
contrast between what survives and what is lost is sharpest. We have
the inscriptional record from the Tophet (thousands of stelae across
six centuries), the archaeological record of sanctuaries and
domestic religious practice (Kerkouane especially), the Greek and
Latin literary record (uneven, hostile-framed, occasionally
preserving real practice through polemic), and the comparative
Phoenician homeland evidence. We do not have a Carthaginian
theological tradition: no surviving prayer book, no narrative of the
gods, no equivalent of the Greek mythological tradition.

This thread walks the religious landscape in the order that supports
honest assessment. Theme prose first; then the four principal
deities with their distinctive features and contested questions;
then the Tophet question with both its theme-level and
institution-level treatments; and finally the coinage event as
evidence of how religion and civic-economic life intertwined.

The thread includes [Bes](/deities/bes) deliberately — the Egyptian-
origin household deity is the principal example of how the
archaeological record differs from the literary record. Bes is
ubiquitous in Punic household contexts but rare in formal
inscriptions; the civic deities (Baal Hammon, Tanit, Melqart, Eshmun)
appear in the literary discussions but the household deities appear
primarily in the archaeology. The contrast is one of the structural
reasons modern reconstructions of Punic religious life depend so
heavily on material evidence.

[Tophet-question thread](/threads/the-tophet-question) is a deeper
