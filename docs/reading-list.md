# Reading list

The bibliography that grounds *Qart-Hadasht*. A living document — additions welcome.

## Tier 1 — modern narrative & synthesis

These are the books to read first if you want a serious overview of Carthage.

- **Hoyos, Dexter.** *Hannibal: A Hellenistic Life.* Yale, 2003.
  Reads Hannibal as a Hellenistic-era general, not the older "alien Semitic enemy of Rome" frame. Strong on military analysis and the Barcid family's role in Carthaginian politics.

- **MacDonald, Eve.** *Carthage: A New History.* Yale, 2015.
  Single-volume modern history grounded in recent archaeology. Foregrounds Carthage as a major Mediterranean power in its own right, not just Rome's antagonist.

- **Miles, Richard.** *Carthage Must Be Destroyed: The Rise and Fall of an Ancient Civilization.* Penguin, 2010.
  Wide-ranging cultural and political history. Particularly strong on Hellenistic-era Carthage and the religious dimensions of the conflict with Rome.

- **Lancel, Serge.** *Carthage: A History.* Trans. Antonia Nevill. Blackwell, 1995.
  By the archaeologist who directed excavations at Carthage for decades. Anchored in physical evidence in ways few general histories are.

- **Quinn, Josephine.** *In Search of the Phoenicians.* Princeton, 2018.
  Argues that "Phoenician" identity is largely a Greek and modern construction — the people we lump together didn't think of themselves that way. Affects how this project models ethnicity and identity.

- **Cooper, Paul M. M.** *Fall of Civilizations: Stories of Greatness and Decline.* Duckworth, 2024.
  Includes a substantial chapter on Carthage. Companion to the podcast of the same name.

## Tier 1 — primary sources

The ancient texts the encyclopedia repeatedly cites. All public-domain English translations linked.

- **Polybius.** *Histories.* Books 1, 3, 6, 14, 15. (Shuckburgh trans., 1889 — [Perseus](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.01.0234))
  The single most reliable surviving source. Greek hostage in Rome with access to Scipionic family testimony.

- **Livy.** *Ab Urbe Condita,* Books 21–30. (Roberts trans., 1912 — [Perseus](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.02.0144))
  Fullest narrative of the Second Punic War. Working ~175 years after the fact, often from Polybius. Rhetorically heightened.

- **Appian.** *Punica* (Roman History, Book 8). (White trans., 1899 — [Perseus](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.01.0230))
  Indispensable for the Third Punic War — our principal continuous source for the destruction of Carthage.

- **Diodorus Siculus.** *Library of History.* Fragmentary but essential for early Carthage and the Sicilian Wars.

- **Justin.** *Epitome of Pompeius Trogus,* Book 18. The Dido foundation tradition.

- **Cornelius Nepos.** *Life of Hannibal.* Short Roman biography.

## Tier 2 — specialist

Add as topics demand.

- Hoyos, Dexter (ed.). *A Companion to the Punic Wars.* Blackwell.
- Goldsworthy, Adrian. *The Fall of Carthage.* Cassell, 2003. (Military focus.)
- Aubet, María Eugenia. *The Phoenicians and the West.* Cambridge, 2nd ed.
- Krings, Véronique (ed.). *La civilisation phénicienne et punique.* Brill. (French; reference handbook.)
- Schwartz, Jeffrey H. et al. — papers on the Tophet and the infant-sacrifice debate.
